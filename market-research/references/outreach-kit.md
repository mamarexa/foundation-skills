# Founder's outreach kit

The agent **prepares** this kit. The **founder** decides whether to use it, and makes every
contact personally, one at a time. The agent never sends these messages, never makes calls,
and never fills in competitors' forms.

This is practical guidance, not legal advice. Rules on recording calls, unsolicited messages
and trade secrets differ by country.

## Pick the approach per question

| Approach | When it fits | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Buy it** | Low-cost products and services sold to the public (a coffee, a monthly software plan, a class) | Become a real customer. It's the most reliable and least intrusive way to learn price, quality and service. |
| **B. Open research** | Anything, especially B2B questions, terms and how the market works | Say who you are: "I'm starting a ___ business and researching the market." Some will decline. The ones who answer give better information. |
| **C. Ordinary customer enquiry** | Questions any member of the public can ask about offers **sold to the public**: standard prices, packages, opening hours, delivery areas, lead times | Ask what a prospective customer would ask, briefly, without claiming a false identity. This is the only "mystery shopping" style approach in the kit, and it has limits (below). |
| **D. Ask the supply side** | Equipment prices, ingredient costs, rent levels, typical margins | Suppliers, landlords, agents and trade associations. You *are* their prospective customer, so this is fully genuine. It's often the best source of industry cost data. |

**Don't use C for:**

- custom quotes, demos, site visits or trials that take real staff time;
- anything the competitor gives only under NDA or to verified business customers;
- any question whose answer is confidential (their costs, margins, supplier terms, customer
  lists).

For those, use B or D instead.

## Rules (share these with the founder)

1. **Keep it short.** A few minutes of a competitor's time at most. Don't book slots that a
   real customer could have had.
2. **No fake transactions.** No placing orders you'll cancel, reserving tables, starting trials
   under invented details, or submitting quote requests for a project that doesn't exist.
3. **No false identity.** Don't invent a company, job title or credentials, and don't sign
   anything in a name that isn't yours. Approach C means asking ordinary questions, not
   pretending to be someone specific.
4. **Nothing confidential.** Don't ask for, or accept, internal information: costs, supplier
   contracts, customer lists or anything marked confidential. That's where legal risk (trade
   secrets) starts.
5. **Recording:** check whether both parties must consent in your location before you record a
   call. When in doubt, take notes instead.
6. **One by one.** Personal one-to-one messages only, never a mailing list or automated
   sequence. Bulk unsolicited email is regulated almost everywhere.
7. **Log it.** Record the date, the channel and what was said, so the benchmark can cite
   "founder enquiry, <date>".

## Templates

The agent fills the `[questions]` and adapts the wording to the business. The founder must
make every sentence true for them before using it.

### Email: open research (B)

> **Subject:** Quick question from a future [local business / peer in the industry]
>
> Hi [business name] team,
>
> I'm [your name], and I'm planning to open a [type of business] in [area]. I'm researching
> the local market before I commit, and I'd appreciate a couple of quick answers if you're
> happy to share them:
>
> 1. [question]
> 2. [question]
>
> No problem at all if you'd rather not. Thanks for your time either way.
>
> [Your name]

### Email: ordinary customer enquiry (C), public offers only

> **Subject:** Question about your [product/service]
>
> Hi,
>
> Could you tell me [standard price / what's included in the ___ package / your delivery area
> and charge / your usual lead time for ___]?
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

Keep it to the questions a real customer would ask, and nothing more.

### Call script: ordinary customer enquiry (C)

> "Hi, I've got a quick question about your [product/service]. What does [standard option]
> cost, and [does it include ___ / how soon could it start]?"
>
> (Listen, note the answer, thank them.) "That's helpful, thanks very much."

Don't stretch the call, and don't ask for a formal quote or a callback from sales.

### Email: supplier / partner enquiry (D)

> **Subject:** Pricing enquiry: [equipment / ingredients / premises] for a new [business]
>
> Hi,
>
> I'm setting up a [business] in [area], opening around [date], and I'm comparing suppliers
> for [item]. Could you send me your standard pricing for [quantity / spec] and your usual
> terms (minimum order, delivery, payment terms)?
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name], [phone]
