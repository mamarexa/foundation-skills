# Worked sizing examples

The numbers below are **illustrative**. They show the method and are not data to reuse. Every
real figure needs a cited source.

## 1. Local business: specialty café, one location

The market is a catchment area, not a country.

- **Catchment:** people within about 10 minutes' walk, plus commuters passing the site.
  - Residents in the radius: 18,000 [census small-area data].
  - Weekday footfall past the site: 6,500/day [council footfall counter, or the founder's own
    count over three mornings].
- **Demand:** 60% of adults drink coffee out of the home at least weekly [national survey];
  average 2.1 purchases/week; average ticket 4.20 [local menu scan].
- **TAM** (catchment spend on out-of-home coffee): 18,000 × 0.60 × 2.1 × 52 × 4.20
  ≈ 4.95M/yr, plus the commuter flow sized the same way.
- **SAM:** the dayparts and formats this café serves (weekday mornings and lunch, takeaway
  plus 30 seats), for example 55% of TAM.
- **SOM (capacity and competition based):** max throughput = 2 baristas × 60 drinks/hour in the
  peak hours. Take the realistic daily transactions (250 in Year 1, rising to 350) × ticket
  × days open. Then check it against the combined capacity of the 4 competing cafés within the
  same radius.
- **Cross-check:** the local share implied by SOM against the number of competitors. With 5
  outlets sharing the catchment, a new entrant taking over 20% in Year 1 needs a clear reason.

## 2. B2B SaaS: scheduling software for dental practices, UK

- **Customer unit:** a dental practice.
- **Count:** about 12,000 practices [NHS/GDC register or a business-register SIC code search].
- **Price:** 1,800/practice/year (the product's middle tier).
- **TAM** = 12,000 × 1,800 = 21.6M/yr.
- **SAM:** practices with 2+ chairs, not locked into a corporate group's in-house system, for
  example 8,000 → 14.4M.
- **SOM (go-to-market capacity):** 2 sales reps × 8 closes/month × 12 = 192 practices/yr, plus
  self-serve sign-ups of 40/month × 12 = 480, less churn. Five-year cumulative customers is then
  about 2,400 (≈ 30% of SAM, which needs justification) or about 1,500 after conservative churn.
  State both.
- **Top-down cross-check:** UK dental practice-management software spend from an industry
  report, narrowed to the scheduling share. If it lands far from 14.4M, revisit the price or
  the practice count.

## 3. Manufacturing: recycled-PET flakes, regional

- **Customer unit:** tonnes of rPET flake bought by converters in the region.
- **TAM:** regional PET packaging consumption [industry association] × mandated or targeted
  recycled content share [regulation] = demand for rPET (tonnes/yr) × price/tonne [price
  index, dated].
- **SAM:** converters within an economic trucking radius that accept this food-grade or
  non-food spec.
- **SOM:** the plant's rated capacity × realistic utilization (40–60% in Year 1). Then check
  that the *supply* side is feasible too: waste-bale availability in the catchment, and
  competing recyclers' capacity.
- For commodity products, the binding constraint is often supply or capacity, not demand.
  Size both.

## Common mistakes

- Using TAM as the market and applying "we'll get 1%".
- Counting the whole country for a location-bound business.
- Mixing units (customers vs. money, one-off vs. per year).
- Stale data. Always show the year of each figure.
- Leaving out substitutes, which makes TAM look uncontested.
